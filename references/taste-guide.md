# Taste guide for launch and customer-overview videos

This skill should produce videos that feel closer to a strong OpenAI / Anthropic launch
presentation than a generic template slideshow.

## Default taste

- Quiet confidence over hype.
- One idea per scene.
- Large, factual typography.
- Product footage, demos, charts, and model behavior as the visual hero.
- Restrained motion: precise transitions, small parallax, purposeful zooms.
- Clear hierarchy: headline, one explanation, one proof object.
- Use brand color as an accent, not a full-screen wash.

## 16:9 customer overview

Use this for external/customer-facing overview videos:

- Horizontal 16:9.
- Fixed two-column layouts for demo/proof sections: text and claims on the left, video/chart on the right.
- Dedicated title card with a single strong title, not a dashboard of many elements.
- Benchmarks should be paired with the relevant demo topic: demo page, then benchmark proof page with similar layout.
- Use zoom-in or crop windows for charts; never rely on a tiny full-page screenshot.
- Keep decorative cards minimal. The content should feel like a keynote artifact, not a marketing landing page.

## OpenAI-inspired cues

- High contrast, generous negative space.
- Calm product-first demos.
- Short declarative claims, backed by live behavior or evidence.
- Smooth linear camera/viewport moves, no frantic cuts.
- Avoid overexplaining UI controls on screen; let narration carry explanation.

## Anthropic-inspired cues

- Editorial restraint.
- Warm neutral surfaces, but avoid beige monotony.
- Human language and clear reasoning.
- Benchmark and safety/comparison content should feel legible and sober.
- Transitions can be softer, with less glow and less chrome.

## Avoid

- Generic SaaS hero sections.
- Full-screen gradient blobs, decorative orbs, and stock-like abstract backgrounds.
- Text-heavy pages where the narration repeats every visible word.
- Reusing the same transition phrase at every section boundary, such as "For X..."
- Benchmark screenshots that are too small to read.
- Splicing final mixed audio from different videos.

## Scene checklist

- [ ] Is there exactly one primary idea?
- [ ] Can the viewer understand the page with the sound off?
- [ ] Does the narration add interpretation rather than read labels?
- [ ] Is the proof object large enough to inspect on a laptop screen?
- [ ] Does the transition feel motivated by the previous claim?
